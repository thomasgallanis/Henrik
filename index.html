Methodology

Analysis before action.

The sequence below describes a working method. It is not a system that guarantees successful trades.

01

Understand

Establish context: the market, the timeframe, the prevailing conditions, and the question being asked.

02

Analyse

Examine structure, price, volume, volatility and the wider fundamental and sentiment picture as analytical inputs.

03

Assess risk

Consider exposure, downside, position size and the conditions under which the idea would be wrong.

04

Act with discipline

If action is taken, it is taken within predefined bounds — not in reaction to noise.

05

Review & adapt

After the fact, review what the market actually did. Adapt the process.

Analytical inputs — not guarantees: market structure, price action, trading volume, volatility, technical indicators, fundamental developments, economic announcements, market sentiment, liquidity, potential catalysts.

Risk

Opportunity begins with understanding risk.

Responsible market participation requires understanding both potential opportunity and potential downside. Markets are uncertain. Volatility is a feature of them.

Position sizing

The size of a position is a risk decision before it is a market decision.

Exposure

Concentration, correlation and overall exposure are examined as part of any considered view.

Volatility

Volatility can expand without notice. Analysis that ignores it is incomplete.

Entry planning

If a market is engaged, the terms of engagement should be defined in advance.

Exit planning

Knowing how a view would be concluded is part of taking it seriously.

Discipline

Process exists to constrain improvisation when conditions become emotionally loud.

Scenario analysis

More than one path is possible. Preparing for alternative outcomes is a form of respect for uncertainty.

Changing market conditions

A reading that was sound yesterday may be incomplete today. Review is continuous.

Financial markets involve risk and past performance is not indicative of future results.

About

A global perspective shaped by markets.

Mike Clyne was born in Germany on 2 February 1989 and raised in Canada. That early movement between countries formed a practical international outlook — one that later became central to his professional life in financial markets.

He now works from London, United Kingdom, as an international financial markets professional. His work is concentrated on active financial-market analysis, day trading, short-term market analysis, financial strategy, market research, and broader business and strategic thinking.

Across 15+ years of studying, analysing and participating in global financial markets, he has built experience in equities, indices, commodities, currencies and digital assets. More than 3,000 individual market analyses and trading setups have been undertaken as part of an ongoing practice of observation, structure and review.

His professional relationships extend to 300+ private clients across 15+ countries. The emphasis is on discretion, clear communication and long-term professional relationships.

He holds a PhD from the University of Cambridge and completed a two-year advanced programme at London Business School. The working philosophy is simple: learn continuously, analyse before acting, respect risk, communicate clearly, and build long-term professional relationships.

Approach

Analysis before action.

A working method for examining markets with discipline. It is not a system that guarantees successful trades.

The method

  1. Understand. Establish context.
  2. Analyse. Examine structure, price, volume, volatility and the wider picture as inputs.
  3. Assess risk. Consider exposure, downside and invalidation.
  4. Act with discipline. If action is taken, it is taken within predefined bounds.
  5. Review & adapt. Markets change; so must the study of them.

Opportunity begins with understanding risk.

Responsible market participation requires understanding both potential opportunity and potential downside. Position sizing, exposure, volatility, entry and exit planning, discipline, scenario analysis and changing market conditions are all part of that examination.
